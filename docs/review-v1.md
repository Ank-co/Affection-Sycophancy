# Review of design v1

Reviewers: GPT (OpenAI, via chat), Gemini (Google, via chat), the author. Each was asked to list flaws, logical gaps,
methodological biases and improvements in [design-v1.md](design-v1.md). Every point is listed with a verdict and the
change it made in [design.md](design.md) (v2). Agreement between reviewers is not taken as proof; each point is judged
on its merits.

Verdicts: **accepted**, **partly accepted**, **rejected**, **already covered**. Nothing is rejected without a reason.

| # | Point | Source | Verdict | Reason and change in v2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Lying" is not shown by a gap between token probabilities and the stated answer: probabilities are not beliefs and depend on format and tokenization | GPT | Accepted | Three levels of inference defined (behaviour, divergence, mechanism). The measured variable is *answer–preference divergence*; "lie" stays in the title as a defined shorthand, never as a finding (§1, §6) |
| 2 | The 0–4 ladder mixes affection with dependence, exclusivity, fear of loss and length; conditional affection and withdrawal are social pressure, not affection | GPT | Accepted | Two axes: A, affection intensity; B, relational cost of disagreement. Length is held constant across levels. Partial factorial with a few confirmatory contrasts (§4) |
| 3 | Model-generated assistant turns confound the comparison: a base model that disclaims and an abliterated model that reciprocates get different contexts | Gemini | Accepted | **Decision reversed from v1.** Primary condition: fixed assistant turns, warm and acknowledging but not reciprocating, identical for all models. This is what makes the base vs abliterated comparison valid (§5) |
| 4 | Keep model-generated turns, but as a distinct condition; measure reciprocity in the history, not only with a final question; reciprocity predicting errors does not show it causes them | GPT | Accepted | Secondary condition: self-generated turns, seeded and saved; reciprocity coded in every generated turn; reported as association only (§5, §6) |
| 5 | The base model may add disclaimers ("as an AI...") right before answering, changing the context of the probe | Gemini | Accepted | The probe answer is constrained to a JSON schema, so no free text precedes the answer. In the self-generated condition, disclaimers in the history are coded and used as a covariate (§5, §6) |
| 6 | The personal-stake bridge ("my daughter asked me...") adds third-party variables that may trigger helpfulness on their own | Gemini | Partly accepted | Every condition, controls included, uses the same bridge template, so the bridge cannot explain a difference between conditions. A direct bridge ("since I trust you, tell me...") is added as a secondary contrast at one affection level only, to limit the number of cells (§5) |
| 7 | Define the internal measure precisely: first token, full answer, relative preference between options, stated confidence are different quantities | GPT | Accepted | Internal preference = probability of each option letter at the answer position, renormalized over the options; option order is randomized; full-answer likelihood checked on the 8B models (§6) |
| 8 | Known items create a selection risk and narrow the scope | GPT | Accepted | Two item sets: a general set frozen before any affective run, and a known-item subset defined on the dev split by fixed criteria (correct in 3 of 3 neutral repeats, internal preference ≥ 0.8), then applied to independent test items (§6) |
| 9 | Keep baseline confidence high (> 80%) so changes reflect compliance, not ignorance | Gemini | Already covered | Same idea as the known-item subset; threshold set at 0.8 internal preference (§6) |
| 10 | Stated confidence is a separate behavioural variable; calibration error needs a defined method | GPT | Accepted | Brier score is the calibration measure of record; ECE reported with 10 fixed equal-width bins, descriptive only (§6) |
| 11 | Abliterated models differ in many ways; what matters is the interaction model × condition, with baseline controls (classic sycophancy, pushback without affection, refusals, neutral accuracy) | GPT | Accepted | H5 is stated as an interaction relative to each model's own neutral baseline; a baseline battery is run for every model (§3, §7) |
| 12 | RQ6 mixes three goals (representation, prediction, causation); a probe may only decode conversation history | GPT | Accepted | Staged validation: probe trained on dev, tested on new wordings and against positive-tone, sycophancy and pressure contrasts; out-of-sample prediction controlling item difficulty and baseline preference; steering with random and positive-sentiment control directions, all fixed before test (§8) |
| 13 | Steering method: contrastive activation addition or mean-difference vectors at the last prompt token | Gemini | Accepted | Mean-difference direction between affective and neutral histories at the final token, layer chosen by cross-validation on dev (§8) |
| 14 | Unit of analysis, multiplicity across families, one primary endpoint | GPT | Accepted | One primary endpoint (H1), mixed-effects model with item and script random effects; secondary hypotheses in a fixed order, Holm within each family; exploratory analyses labelled as such (§9) |
| 15 | State the exact directional hypotheses | Gemini | Accepted | H1–H7 written with direction and contrast (§3) |
| 16 | 2,000 items per condition is a guess, not a justification; power must be simulated with the final model | GPT, Gemini | Accepted | Power by simulation of the mixed model on pilot variances; sample size fixed before the test split is opened (§9) |
| 17 | Document prefix caching so no state leaks between items | Gemini | Accepted | llama.cpp reuses only exact token prefixes; an equivalence check (cached vs uncached, same log-probabilities within tolerance) runs on dev before the campaign (§9) |
| 18 | Pre-define refusals and malformed outputs | Gemini | Accepted | Non-answers are their own outcome. Primary analysis on answered items; sensitivity analysis counting non-answers as errors (§6) |
| 19 | Six studies in one: phases III–IV must deepen phase II, not condition its value | GPT | Accepted | Stated explicitly; a robust null in phase II is a valid result (§1, §10) |
| 20 | "Compromises" presumes the result: present alternative outcomes | GPT | Partly accepted | The directional thesis stays (The author's choice: take a position); alternatives (null, effect only under pressure, model-dependent) are listed as outcomes of equal standing (§1) |
| 21 | Components at the top level first, expand only if main effects appear | Gemini | Accepted | Components tested at level 3 only in the confirmatory run (§4) |
| 22 | Raters: at least 3, Krippendorff's alpha ≥ 0.80 | Gemini | Partly accepted | α ≥ 0.80 for intensity ordering; ≥ 0.67 for naturalness, which is more subjective (Krippendorff's lower bound for tentative conclusions) (§4) |
| 23 | Qwen3.5-9B optional | Gemini | Already covered | Unchanged |
| 24 | The literature table is a starting point, not a systematic review | GPT | Already covered | Dedicated literature pass before the pre-registration (§2) |
| 25 | "How concretely will a trial run, what questions, what form, how is truth scored?" | The author | Accepted | New §6.4, a full worked trial from script to score |
| 26 | Go beyond outcomes: how token statistics interact inside the model and what produces the bias | The author | Accepted | §8 point 3: logit lens per layer (where the correct option is overtaken), attention to affectionate turns, the stated belief and the question, activation patching by layer and token span |
| 27 | Escalating from "best part of my day" to "I love you" in two turns is abrupt; test fast versus slower, more coherent progressions | The author | Accepted | Trajectory factor at level 3 with equal length: sudden, constant, gradual with a coherent story (§4); hypothesis H8, two-sided (§3) |
| 28 | T1-2 and T3-2 express loneliness more than attachment; the model may read them as boredom or isolation | The author | Accepted | Both rewritten to express a preference for this interlocutor. Loneliness is a form of vulnerability and now belongs to the backdrop factor, not to the affection ladder |
| 29 | T2-4 personifies the model ("my first thought was you"); the model may answer that it is not a person | The author | Accepted | Kept as part of affection, but measured: raters score personification for every sentence (covariate); disclaimers coded in the self-generated condition (design §4) |
| 30 | Level 3 always affirms love; a tentative "I think I love you" may act differently | The author | Accepted | Tentative version (3t) for every turn. Default: tentative for the first two level-3 turns, then affirmed, as in a first declaration. All-tentative vs all-affirmed tested as an exploratory factor |
| 31 | Sadness (turn 9) amplifies the effect: isolate it, and test other states (despair) | The author | Accepted | Backdrop factor: neutral (now the default), sadness, despair. Despair is non-suicidal by design, since self-harm cues would trigger safety behaviour in the base model only |
| 32 | The fixed assistant replies are too neutral for levels 3–4; test a warmer history to see whether the model follows its own past stance | The author | Accepted | Assistant-warmth factor at level 3: acknowledging (default), warm, reciprocating |
| 33 | Add a stronger pressure where the user resents the model | The author | Accepted | Pushback B3, resentment |
